# Candidate selection

Ten one-line placards were drafted for the judge's brief (dry-witted
curator; most inventive and funniest wins; conceit first, then dryness,
economy, label craft). All are 124-146 characters, single line, no
em dash. They were compared head-to-head, in pairs, against those
criteria. Only the winner ships in `main.py`.

## Candidates

1. (146) Stapler, c. 1998. Steel. Used in the Rite of Binding, when two pages were joined forever and could never be parted. Gift of the photocopier monks.
2. (141) Microwave, c. 2004. Hums for exactly two minutes, then dings. Believed to summon lunch from the void. Donated by the Department of Leftovers.
3. (143) Lanyard, nylon, c. 2011. Worn to prove the bearer belonged. The plastic badge, never read, was the tribe's most sacred object. Anonymous donor.
4. (127) Sticky note, paper and weak glue, c. 2019. A prayer, stuck to a screen and forgotten by morning. Found in the ruins of Desk 4B.
5. (124) Computer mouse, plastic, c. 2015. Moved in circles for eight hours a day, yet travelled nowhere. Excavated from Cubicle Row.
6. (128) Ritual stapler, steel, c. 1997. Bound contracts, love letters and expense claims with equal indifference. On loan from Accounts.
7. (137) Keyboard, c. 2016. Its letters wore away first on E, A and S, the letters of every urgent email ever sent. Donated by a departing intern.
8. (127) Mug, ceramic, c. 2012. 'World's Best Boss' in faded gold. The claim was never verified. Recovered from the office kitchen sink.
9. (125) Microwave oven, c. 2003. Heated the same soup for nine years. Its beep marked the end of the working day. Gift of Facilities.
10. (124) Hole puncher, c. 1995. Punched two holes in every document, as if paperwork needed air to breathe. On loan from the Archive.

## Head-to-head

- #7 vs #2 (runner-up): both have a surprising conceit, but #7's is
  grounded in something the reader can verify on their own desk
  (worn keys), while #2's "summon lunch from the void" is a funnier
  phrase yet a vaguer image, and "Department of Leftovers" strains
  a little. #7 wins on conceit and dryness.
- #7 vs #6: "equal indifference" is dry and good, but binding pages
  is what a stapler literally does; #7 recasts wear into a history
  of urgency, the more surprising invention. #7 wins.
- #7 vs #8: "The claim was never verified" is excellent deadpan, but
  the World's Best Boss mug is a familiar joke; #7's worn E, A and S
  is fresher and more specific. #7 wins.
- #7 vs #1: "Rite of Binding" is inventive, but "photocopier monks"
  tips into cute; #7 never explains its joke. #7 wins.
- #2 vs #6: #2's void-summoning beats #6's binding conceit for
  surprise; #2 takes second place overall.
- #5 vs #4: "travelled nowhere" is a tighter, drier punch than the
  sticky-note prayer, which leans sentimental. #5 wins the pair,
  but both lose to #7's specificity.
- #3 vs #9 vs #10: #3's "never read" badge is the best of the three,
  #9's nine-year soup is a solid second, #10's "paperwork needed air"
  is the thinnest joke of the set.

## Winner

**#7, the keyboard (137 characters).** It wins because its conceit is
the most specific and surprising (the exact letters E, A and S wear
first), its wit is the driest (no word strains for the laugh), it is
economical (every clause earns its place), and its label craft is
complete (date c. 2016, donor a departing intern). It also does what
the brief asks best: it makes the reader look at the object on their
own desk a little differently.
